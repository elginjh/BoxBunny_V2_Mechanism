Structural Layout & Fabrication

This page details the physical construction of the custom telescopic lift column — the materials selected, the wall thickness rationale, the Delrin bearing interfaces, and the gusseted joints where aluminium extrusion meets welded steel structure.

Column Components

The telescopic column comprises four primary elements:

Component Material Function
Moving inner member 8080 aluminium extrusion Carries the upper body; slides vertically within the outer tube
Guide surfaces Delrin (acetal) wear pads Low-friction sacrificial interface between inner and outer members
Fixed outer sleeve Welded steel tube with integrated plate Provides structural envelope; resists lateral loads and overturning moments
Axial lifting device HK2T screw jack (travelling nut) Carries axial load; provides height adjustment; self-locking

SHS Wall Thickness (≥ 6 mm)

Large SHS members in the height-adjustment column are specified with a minimum wall thickness of 6 mm. This sizing is driven by:

  • Lateral bending resistance: The outer tube must resist significant bending moments from punch forces applied at the torso height. Thicker walls increase the second moment of area, reducing deflection under lateral loading.
  • Weld joint integrity: Adequate wall thickness ensures full-penetration welds at plate-to-tube intersections, avoiding cold welds or burn-through that compromise joint strength.
  • Bearing surface quality: The inner face of the outer tube serves as one of the bearing surfaces for the Delrin wear pads. Sufficient wall thickness ensures this surface remains flat and does not distort under localised loads.
  • Bolt connection capacity: Mounting plates and brackets are bolted through the SHS wall; thicker walls provide better thread engagement and bolt pull-through resistance.

Delrin Bearing Interfaces

Delrin (acetal/polyoxymethylene) wear pads are used as the bearing interface between the 8080 aluminium inner member and the steel outer tube. Delrin was selected for several reasons:

  • Low friction coefficient against both aluminium and steel (~0.2–0.35 dry), enabling smooth sliding without lubrication.
  • Self-lubricating: Delrin has inherent lubricity, reducing maintenance requirements for the height-adjustment mechanism.
  • Sacrificial wear: Delrin is softer than both aluminium and steel, so wear concentrates on the replaceable pads rather than on the structural members. Pads can be replaced during maintenance without reworking the column.
  • Dimensional stability: Low moisture absorption and good creep resistance ensure consistent clearances over time.
  • Easy machining: Delrin machines cleanly and can be fabricated to precise dimensions with standard tooling.

The wear pads are positioned at the top and bottom of the outer tube, providing a wide moment arm for resisting tilting of the inner member. This arrangement maximises lateral stiffness while minimising the number of bearing surfaces.

Gusseted Aluminium-to-Steel Joints

Where the 8080 aluminium extrusion meets the welded steel structure, gusseted corner brackets are used to create robust, stiff connections. These joints are critical because:

  • Dissimilar metals: Aluminium-to-steel joints cannot be welded without specialised processes. Bolted connections with gusset plates provide a reliable, reversible joint method.
  • Load transfer: Punching forces create large bending moments at the column-to-base interface. Gussets distribute these loads over a wider area, preventing stress concentration at individual bolt holes.
  • Stiffness: Gusseted corners are significantly stiffer than simple L-brackets, reducing angular deflection at the joint under dynamic loading.
  • Distortion prevention: Welded plates in the steel structure can distort due to residual welding stresses. Gussets bridge across potential distortion zones, maintaining alignment between the aluminium and steel components.

Fabrication Notes

A light-shock service factor of approximately 1.5 was adopted because the robot may be adjusted in public demonstration environments with minor bumps and start-stop transients. Specific fabrication considerations:

  • Welded plates are sized to avoid distortion — thicker plates and staggered weld sequences are used where flatness is critical.
  • Internal clearances between inner and outer members are controlled to prevent jamming while maintaining acceptable play for smooth operation.
  • The screw jack is mounted with alignment features that ensure the jack's thrust axis remains coincident with the column centreline, preventing eccentric loading.
